When an important collaboration is under strain

Some professional collaborations are too important to leave to avoidance, misunderstanding or repeated conflict.

I offer conversations for people in an important collaboration where communication, trust or shared responsibility has come under pressure.

This may be relevant when the collaboration still matters, but the usual ways of speaking together are no longer enough.

When the collaboration still matters

A strained collaboration can affect more than the people directly involved.

It can influence decisions, timing, confidence, energy and the wider business, project or organisation around it. Often, those involved know that something needs attention, but it has become difficult to speak clearly without making the situation more fixed.

The purpose of the work is not to create a polished agreement or decide who is right. It is to slow the conversation down, understand what has become difficult, and clarify whether a more workable way of cooperating is possible.

What may bring you here

These conversations may be useful when:

  • the same misunderstandings keep returning
  • trust has become weakened
  • roles, responsibility or expectations have become unclear
  • important conversations are being avoided
  • decisions are becoming harder because the collaboration is under strain
  • the people involved need a calmer setting to speak honestly and listen more carefully

The work can also be relevant where someone around the collaboration, such as an owner, adviser, board member or investor, can see that an important professional connection needs support before it deteriorates further.

How the conversations work

The conversations provide a structured but human setting where the people involved can speak and be heard.

We look at what has happened, how the situation is experienced from different sides, and what each person may need to take responsibility for now.

The aim is not to remove all tension. Some tension belongs in important work. The aim is to make the conversation clearer, more honest and more useful, so that the people involved can better understand what is possible from here.

A clarifying first conversation

The first meeting is a clarifying conversation.

It gives the people involved a chance to describe the situation, meet me, and sense whether this is the right setting for the work.

For conversations like this to be useful, there needs to be enough contact, respect and trust between me and the people taking part. If that fit is not there, it is better to know early. That is not a failure. It is part of understanding what kind of support is needed.
