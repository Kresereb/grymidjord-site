Individual psychotherapy

There may be times in life when something begins to take up more space than it used to. Something may feel heavier, more unsettled, or harder to understand on your own. It may be a particular situation, a relationship, a prolonged strain, or a quieter sense that something in life no longer holds together in the same way.

Individual psychotherapy can be a place to pause and look more closely at what is going on. Not to find quick answers, but to gain a calmer and clearer view of what has become difficult and of what may be calling for change.

The conversations unfold at a pace where there is room both for what is already clear and for what only gradually becomes possible to put into words.

It may, for example, be about

It may be periods of stress or strain, life crises, anxiety or unease, loneliness, difficulties in relationships, dependency, or the experience of being stuck in patterns that have become hard to change on your own.

Some people come with something very specific. Others come with a less defined sense that something in life has become harder to carry, understand, or be in.

It may also be about ambivalence, motivation, or patterns that over time have taken up more space than you want them to.

It does not have to be completely clear beforehand what the problem is. What matters is not having the right words from the beginning, but that there is something calling for attention.

How I work

I work calmly and attentively, with respect for the fact that what is difficult cannot always — and should not always — be simplified.

The conversations take their starting point in what you bring. They may concern what is current here and now, but also patterns, experiences, or relationships that over time have come to matter for how you are and how you feel in your life.

The aim is not to force anything forward, but to create a space where it becomes possible to understand more of what is at stake and to find a more sustainable way of being with it.
